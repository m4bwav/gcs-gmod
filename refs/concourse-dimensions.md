- Floor 275 x 120 ft, vault crown about 125 ft (commonly published figures; Wikipedia "Grand Central Terminal").

- Vault modelled as a half circle of radius 60 ft (720 u) springing at 65 ft (780 u). The real vault is flatter (an elliptical barrel); check against a section drawing before detailing.
- Balconies at 26 ft, 22 ft deep; three end-wall windows 22 ft wide. These are guesses from photos, to be replaced by measured values (HABS drawings, HABS NY-5450, are the source to find next).

## Lower-level tracks (100-117), blockout facts (2026-11-25, approximate, from photos and general sources)
- Lower (suburban) level sits about 28-30 ft (8.5-9 m) below the upper track level; dining concourse is at this level.
- Gates on the dining concourse north side: marble surround, carved oak-leaf lunette over a bronze-framed doorway, single pendant globe lamp, serif gilt-black lettering "108 TRACKS 107" (refs: Tracks_107_and_108..., Awesome_track_number...).
- Behind the gate a narrow ramp/walkway leads down to island platforms about 15-20 ft (4.5-6 m) wide; high-level platforms, light grey concrete/terrazzo tile floor, tubular steel railings.
- Ceiling about 12-15 ft (3.7-4.5 m) above platform: the upper track deck's steel frame, dark riveted girders and concrete, columns at roughly 15-20 ft spacing along the platform edges.
- Lighting: continuous linear fluorescent/LED strips along the platform centre, plus hanging blue-and-white Metro-North track and departure signs; walls dark grey concrete, white-painted soffits near gates.
- Plan: RailUS_GCT_lowerTracksPlan.jpg (Wilgus, PD) shows the suburban-level loop and track fan.

## Lexington and Graybar passages, blockout facts (2026-11-25, approximate, from photos)
- Graybar Passage: runs north-south off the east end of the main concourse toward the Graybar Building; about 30-35 ft (9-10.5 m) wide, crown roughly 25-30 ft; a run of cross-vaulted (groined) bays split by wide flattened-arch ribs springing from pilasters with carved capitals (GCT_Graybar_Passage.jpg). Ceiling cream plaster; the painted mural vaults are the "Graybar Passage mural" refs.
- Graybar walls: buff Botticino-like marble/stone in ashlar courses; floor warm tan terrazzo/marble, polished. Lamps: brass-and-glass chandeliers hung on long rods, one per bay, plus a large pierced bronze ring fixture. Free-standing glass-box retail kiosks with metal canopies sit mid-floor; small brass "GRAYBAR" plaque sign.
- Lexington Passage: runs east from the concourse level toward Lexington Ave (Passageway_to_Lexington_ave_lines.jpg); about 20-25 ft (6-7.5 m) wide, low flat ceiling about 10-12 ft with shallow coffer/beam bands and recessed downlights plus small brass-rimmed glass pendant domes.
- Entry: segmental (flattened) marble arch with incised black serif "LEXINGTON PASSAGE" above; pink-beige polished marble cladding on walls and pilasters, beige stone tile floor in large rectangles.
- Shop fronts: continuous glazing in bronze/dark metal frames set flush in the marble, a dark louvre/vent band above each front; tenants' own signs inside the glass; navy blade sign with gold GCT monogram + "LEXINGTON PASSAGE" at the mouth; red EXIT boxes.
- No Commons photo of the Lexington Ave street entrance itself was found.

## Grand Central Market, blockout facts (2026-11-25, estimates from photos)
- Hall: one long straight corridor-hall running east-west along the north side of 43rd St between the Lexington Passage end of the terminal and Lexington Ave; estimate about 240 ft (73 m) long, 25-30 ft (7.5-9 m) wide, flat ceiling about 14-16 ft (4.3-5 m), rising at the centre into a shallow oval dome/cove holding the bronze-and-crystal "tree root" chandelier (GCT_Market_2.jpg).
- West (terminal) entrance: off the passage level behind a marble balustrade landing; a segmental-arched opening in buff marble with bronze-framed glass doors, carved serif "GRAND CENTRAL MARKET" above, and a hanging green dome-shaped sign with gold lettering (GCT_Market.jpg).
- Street entrance: on Lexington Ave/43rd St, a tall glazed bay of bronze-framed doors under a marquee canopy with "GRAND CENTRAL MARKET" lettering, set in grey limestone with a bronze eagle above; navy vertical "MARKET" banners (GCT_Market_3.jpg).
- Layout: a single central aisle about 8-10 ft wide; open vendor counters line both long walls (refrigerated glass display cases, wood-faced counters, shelves behind), no partitions between stalls (Grand_Central_Mart_Lex_Av_food_corridor_jeh.jpg).
- Materials and colours: cream/buff plaster ceiling and soffits, buff marble at entrances, floor large beige-and-tan checker tile; warm yellow-amber light, vendor colours accents (blue, green, red).
- Lamps: rows of hanging brushed-metal drum/cone pendants down the aisle, track spotlights along both counter lines, the chandelier at the dome; signage is per-vendor oval hanging signs (Zaro's, Ceriello) and painted boards.

## The Campbell (Campbell Apartment bar), blockout facts (2026-11-25, estimates from photos)
- Room: one tall rectangular hall, about 60 ft long x 30 ft wide (18 x 9 m), about 25 ft (7.6 m) high (published figures; treat as estimates). Reached from Vanderbilt Ave at 43rd St via a side door and stair, or from the terminal's southwest corner by a short stair/corridor; a mezzanine balcony with bronze quatrefoil-roundel railing runs across one end (GCT_Campbell_3.jpg).
- Ceiling: flat timber ceiling of close dark beams, every beam and the deep frieze below painted in Florentine-style polychrome (ochre, red, green, gold shields and scrolls); carved gilt corbel brackets on wall piers carry it (GCT_Campbell_1.jpg).
- Window: one huge leaded window filling most of a long wall, about 20 ft tall x 18 ft wide, dark wood mullions in a 6-column x 3-tier grid of small rounded-rectangle panes, frosted cream glass, backlit warm (Campbell_Apartment_nyc1.jpg).
- Fireplace: centred on the far short wall; stone mantel about 8 ft wide with carved frieze, black firebox, tall tapering plaster hood/chimney-breast with a winged relief figure rising to the frieze. A Gothic arched carved-wood grille sits beside it, a plain wood door below (GCT_Campbell_1.jpg).
- Bar: long straight counter under the leaded window, about 35-40 ft, green marble top, dark wood front with backlit pierced quatrefoil fretwork panels, back bar is a low lit bottle shelf on the window sill; tall leather-backed stools with nailhead trim.
- Materials and colours: walls rough gold-tan travertine-look plaster in panels framed by painted geometric bands, dark wood wainscot, dark herringbone oak floor, deep green velvet banquette down the opposite long wall with small round dark wood tables, a dark rug and club chairs centre.
- Lamps: paired wall sconces on tall brass rods, with cream-and-red pleated tapered shades with fringes, two per pier; warm amber low light overall.

## Grand Central Madison (LIRR, opened 2023), blockout facts (2026-11-26)
Sourced (en.wikipedia "Grand Central Madison", read 2026-11-26): station area 700,000 sq ft (65,000 m2), of it 120,000 sq ft passenger space and 25,000 sq ft retail; platforms more than 140 ft (43 m) below Park Avenue; four escalator banks (under 45th, 46th, 47th, 48th St) up to 180 ft (55 m) long descending more than 90 ft (27 m), one bank holds more than five escalators, one is the longest in NYC; 47 escalators and 22 elevators in total; two caverns, each with two levels, each level two tracks on one island platform (8 tracks, 4 platforms); mezzanine tiling and lights colour-coded per bank (45th light green, 46th blue, 47th purple); Madison Concourse art: "River Light" mosaic 80 ft (24 m), Kusama mosaic 120 ft (37 m).
Estimates from photos:
- Concourse (Grand_Central_Madison_Opening_Day_Middle_Concourse.jpg): long straight hall, about 40-50 ft wide, 12-14 ft flat ceiling of white panels with recessed round downlights and long linear light slots; light grey terrazzo floor with dark grey bands; stainless/white wall panels, shopfront glazing one side, the colourful Kusama mosaic wall the other. Bright cool-white even light.
- Escalator shaft (Grand_Central_Madison_escalators.jpg): three to four escalators side by side in a tube-like barrel-vaulted white ceiling with round downlights, frosted glass balustrades, stainless decks; slope about 30 deg; shaft about 35-40 ft wide.
- Platform (Tracks_203_and_204_...jpg): island platform about 25-30 ft wide, curved white ribbed-panel walls and a low curved ceiling with linear LED troughs; yellow edge strips; grey granite-look floor; escalators/stairs drop through the platform centre with stainless rails; digital signs hung from ceiling.

## Track 61 / M42 under the Waldorf, blockout facts (2026-11-26)
Sourced (en.wikipedia "Track 61 (New York City)", read 2026-11-26): Track 61 is a storage track in the 12-track "Lex Yard" under the Waldorf Astoria, northeast of the terminal; shares a platform with Track 63 to its west (another platform between Tracks 53/54); built 1910s for the power and heating plant, platforms first used to carry ashes away; key feature a large freight elevator sized for an automobile, rising to the hotel garage (FDR's armoured car drove off the train onto the platform and into it). The car long parked on Track 63 (MNCW #002) is a baggage car, NOT FDR's; moved to the Danbury Railway Museum in 2019 (GCT_FDR_Train_Car_... photo shows it there, outdoors).
No free-licence photo of the Track 61 platform or the elevator was found on Commons; look below is from widely published photos and is an estimate:
- Platform: plain concrete, low, about 10-12 ft wide, exposed riveted steel columns and beams overhead, grimy whitewashed/brick walls, sparse bare bulbs and cage lamps, very dim; elevator a large steel-door freight shaft at the platform end.
- M42 substation (GCT_M42_Basement_*.jpg, sourced as the M42 room): long hall about 30-40 ft wide, painted grey concrete floor with steel grates, rows of grey modern switchgear cabinets, old riveted steel beams, stained concrete/brick walls with high small windows, one preserved black 1910s rotary converter on a plinth; bright cool LED fixtures.
